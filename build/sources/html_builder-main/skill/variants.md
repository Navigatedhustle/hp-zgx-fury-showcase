## A
**Cinematic and highly interactive.** Pick the ONE interaction that makes this slide's message self-evident
(plan idea: see "idea_a") and build it with a scene from the `H.scene` catalogue — `build`, `compare`,
`calculator`, `chartFocus`, `explorer` or `timeline`. Choose by what the content *is*: two states of the world →
`compare`; a number that depends on the audience's numbers → `calculator`; one series carrying the point →
`chartFocus`; parallel items with detail behind them → `explorer`; an ordered process → `timeline`; a sequence of
claims → `build`.

The scene owns the step machine, the reset, the reduced-motion path and keeping the control live after the last
step. You own the composition, the copy, the data and one short caption per step — the line you would say out loud
at that moment. Do not hand-write a step machine when a scene fits: that is what used to make these slides worse
than the calm ones, not better.

Count up the numbers that matter. Every control must be a real `<button>` or `<input>` and must still work once
the steps are spent. Motion must explain, not decorate. Make it feel like the best slide in an HP keynote.

## B
**Creative, refined, minimal interaction.** Editorial layout with strong hierarchy and the full HP polish
(plan idea: see "idea_b"). Interaction is limited to `.appear` reveals (0–5 steps) and `data-count` count-ups.
Leave `<script>` empty unless a chart must be drawn. The final step must read perfectly as a printed page.
Make it a different, calmer design than a cinematic version would be — not the same layout minus the JS.
